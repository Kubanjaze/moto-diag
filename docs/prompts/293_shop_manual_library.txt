- **What it ships** (the brief's outline, with K119 and K121 to K123):
  1. **One migration.** `manual_references` gains:
     - the shop that owns the file, the file's sha256 and its size in bytes, who uploaded it, and its page count;
     - its review state: `status` (`pending_review`, `approved`, `rejected`, `taken_down`, a `Literal` at every contract boundary), `reviewed_by`, `reviewed_at` and `review_reason`.
     A new table, `manual_pages` (the manual, the page number, the image's path, the page's text), gets an FTS5 index. The rollback follows the `rollback_to_version` pattern. The migration goes live through the deploy skill, under 405's rule.
  2. **The routes,** scoped to a shop like `photos.py`:
     - the list, by make, model and year (`GET /v1/shop/{shop_id}/manuals`);
     - a page (`…/manuals/{id}/pages/{n}`) and search (`…/manuals/search?q`);
     - the upload (`POST …/manuals`), for the shop's admin roles, which the plan names from `SHOP_ROLES`.
     A background job turns each page into an image and a thumbnail and takes its text, with a permissively licensed library such as `pypdfium2`, not PyMuPDF, which is AGPL.
  3. **The review (K119; the draft's section 3).** Until a file passes it, no one sees it except in the uploading shop's own list.
     - **The server's check:**
       - a real PDF, by its contents, not its name;
       - at most 300 MB, and within the plan's storage;
       - not locked by a password;
       - no scripts, attachments or launch actions;
       - a virus scan;
       - its pages render and its text comes out;
       - a file the shop already has approved, with the same fingerprint, is not stored twice.
       A file that fails is refused, with the reason. With no scanner on the machine, every upload is refused; none is let through unscanned.
     - **A person at Mekno** approves the file, fixes its tag and approves it, or rejects it with a reason the shop sees. Every decision records who made it and when.
     - **After approval,** any member can send a page back for review, and a complaint takes the file down until it is decided.
     The read routes return approved files only. The uploader's own list also shows its pending and rejected files, with the reasons.
  4. **The storage limit (K119, K123):**
     - Individual 2 GB, Shop 20 GB, Company 50 GB, and at most 300 MB a file;
     - it counts the bytes of the files the shop uploaded, not the page images or thumbnails made from them;
     - one new limit, `manual_storage_bytes`, read from `TIER_LIMITS`;
     - over it, the upload is refused with how much is used and how to free space, and nothing is charged.
  5. **The backup (K121).** The uploaded manuals' folder joins the nightly backup's upload folders, and each file is sent once, by its content hash. There is no separate weekly backup. The page images are either backed up too or made again from the files after a restore. The plan says which, and a restore test holds it.
  6. **The terms (K119, K122).** The upload clause (the draft's section 2) ships in this phase as the next version of the terms, with the privacy policy's changes. A lawyer's read becomes a later version, as it did for 334 (K84). These change together:
     - both texts in `docs/legal/`;
     - `TERMS_VERSION` and `TERMS_DATE`;
     - the site's pages, made by `scripts/site_build.py legal`.
  7. **The wiring guard.** A test that each new route is reachable, and the `motodiag.reference` entries removed from the allowlist.
  8. **The tests:**
     - another shop can never list, open or search a shop's file, approved or not (the main risk);
     - no one but the reviewer can open a file waiting for review;
     - search finds a torque line;
     - refused: a renamed non-PDF, a PDF with JavaScript, a locked PDF, a file over 300 MB, and a file over the plan's storage;
     - the limit counts the uploaded files, not their page images;
     - a restore brings the manuals back.
  9. **The app's copy of the API.** The new routes change the API. So the app's snapshot, `api-schema/openapi.json` in moto-diag-mobile, is refreshed on that repository's branch of the same name, never `main`, as 403 allows (K84).
