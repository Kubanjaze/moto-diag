## S0-1: the plan's nouns in `src/` and mobile. An extension; no new front door

Vocabulary: `tire_service`, `brake_service`, `suspension_service`,
`drivetrain_service`, `TPMS`, `DOT date`, `chain slack`/`chain tension`,
`belt tension`/`final drive oil`/`shaft drive`, `bleed`. Searched over
`src/` and over `../moto-diag-mobile/src`.

- **The four categories already exist.** `WorkflowCategory` in
  `workflows/models.py` has had `TIRE_SERVICE`, `BRAKE_SERVICE`,
  `SUSPENSION_SERVICE` and `DRIVETRAIN_SERVICE` since Phase 114. That is
  the only file in `src/` naming them, and no template uses any of them.
- **The front door exists.** `motodiag workflow list/show`
  (`cli/workflow.py`) renders any seeded template. Its `--category` choice
  is built from the enum, so `--category tire_service` and the three
  others already parse.
- **Adjacent, not competing:** `advanced/data/service_interval_templates.json`
  (44 rows: make, model pattern, item slug, mileage and month interval,
  e.g. "Primary chain tension adjustment" every 2,500 mi) is a
  maintenance-schedule table read by the scheduler, not a procedure. The
  TPMS, chain, belt and bleed hits elsewhere in `src/` are `known_issues`
  seed rows, recall and TSB data, and the hardware compatibility matrix.
  None is checklist content, and none is reachable as a service protocol.
- **Mobile: zero hits** for the four categories or for
  `workflow template`.

**Decision: extension, no reshape, no new module**, as in 260: one
content migration seeding four templates, plus tests. Every row is a
checklist. The operator's example of a fork, "one row that is not a
checklist at all", did not occur: see S0-4.

## S0-3: the evidence census

**Method.** Every `*.pdf` under `~/research/motodiag` minus the venvs: **260
files**. 157 read fully, 73 partially (searched over the pages they
yield), 16 do not open (F127's debris) and 14 are image-only. That is 244
files with pages and 26,228 pages in all, the same parse 260 recorded.
Every search lower-cases each page and **strips all whitespace before
matching**. Every negative states its vocabulary, scope, count and a
control that hits the exact page the content cites.

**Vocabulary in the makers' own words** (the 260 lesson). Honda, KTM and
Piaggio say "abnormal wear" / "uneven wear", not "cupping". BMW calls
TPMS "RDC" / "tyre pressure control". KTM says "chain sliding guard" and
"link fork". Yamaha writes "drive belt slack" and "final transmission
oil". BMW writes "rear-wheel drive" and "Paralever". Each subject was
searched in those words first.

### Row 269: brake service

Pads: KTM OM pp. 102/108 (≥ 1 mm), 104 (push pistons back, watch the
reservoir; change linings in pairs); F800R RM pp. 94–95 (1.0 mm friction
only); Yamaha Zuma 125 2009 SM (`pdf/yamaha_zuma125_2009_sm.pdf`)

(2.5 / 3.5 mm standard models); PCX SM p. 371 (3.0 mm, warpage 0.30 mm);
Zuma SM p. 119 (3.5 mm, deflection 0.15 mm); Kymco People S 250 SM
(`pdf/kymco_people_s250_sm.pdf`) p. 184 (3.0 mm, runout 0.30 mm);
Beverly p. 196 (3.5 mm, axial run-out 0.1 mm). Caliper: Zuma SM pp.
145–147 (replace piston and dust seals whenever disassembled; no

(cylinder I.D. and piston O.D. limits); Kymco pp. 193–194; Beverly
p. 193. Master cylinder: PCX SM pp. 373–374 (limits; the parts as a
set); Kymco p. 191; Beverly p. 201. Fluid: KTM OM pp. 102, 170 (DOT 4 /
DOT 5.1; never DOT 5); CB500F OM pp. 49, 57 (2 years; paint);
PCX SM p. 363 (sealed container, no mixing); Beverly pp. 48–49
(hygroscopic; 20,000 km or two years); Zuma SM p. 89 (water lowers the
boiling point). Bleeding: PCX SM pp. 363, 367 (when opened or spongy;
the lever-and-valve sequence; 5.4 N·m); Zuma SM pp. 91–92; Kymco
p. 192. Reassembly: caliper bolts KTM OM p. 69 (25 N·m), F800R RM
p. 106 (30 N·m), PCX SM p. 21 (30 N·m, ALOC bolts replaced), Kymco
p. 195 (29–35 N·m); bedding-in F800R RM pp. 67, 106.

The PCX SM prints its disc warpage limit as "0.30 mm (0.001 in)". The
inch conversion is the manual's own error (0.30 mm is 0.012 in), so the
content cites millimetres only.

## S0-4: is any row not a checklist? No fork

The operator asked Step 0 to stop on a real fork, for example a row that
is not a checklist at all. Each row was tested against that:

- **261**: every subject is an inspection or fitting step. The
  wear-pattern names are a vocabulary gap (N1). The item teaches the
  makers' causes and names no pattern it cannot cite.
- **269**: a service procedure in order (pads, discs, caliper, master
  cylinder, fluid, bleed, reassembly).
- **270**: "spring rate selection" looked like a calculation. KTM makes it
  a table lookup by rider weight, so it is a step. "Rear shock rebuild"
  splits the makers: KTM publishes a shock service; Honda forbids opening
  the damper. The item states both, per machine.
- **271**: chain, belt and shaft are three drive types, and a machine has
  one. Every subject has maker support except the u-joint inspection
  (N2), which ships as a no-figure step, the 260 precedent. **Decided,
  not asked:** one template with each drive type's items marked
  optional. The alternative, three templates, would ship the same items
  under three slugs, so it is a presentation choice, not a fork (the
  substrate's `required` flag already means "does not apply to every
  machine", as 259's leak-down item uses it).

cites BMW boxers because they are the documents held, not because a
shaft needs an engine.
