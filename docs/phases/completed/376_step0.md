## S0-2: what the code and the sources add

1. **A journal crediting A/R in a name is an open credit** in QuickBooks
   until the shop applies it in Receive payment (Q2 in the sources). The
   claim's own receivable is itself a journal, so a settlement journal
   against it is matched by hand in either design.
2. **Intuit's import page says nothing on two A/R names in one journal**
   (Q1). Every journal the export writes today has exactly one A/R line.
3. **Xero imports credit notes from a template of their own** (X1), with
   no column linking the invoice they credit: allocation is by hand (X3).
   So the Xero export becomes two files when a range holds settlements.
4. **The Xero credit-note page does not give the sign of `TaxAmount`**
   (X1). Credit-note amounts are negative; the tax column's sign is not
   stated. This is a format gap (Q5 below).
5. **Xero's own bad-debt page** (X5) books a write-off as a credit note to
   a bad-debts account applied to the invoice, and leaves the tax rate to
   the accountant.
6. **Massachusetts: tax not collected stays on the return** (T1: "bad
   debts may not be subtracted from gross receipts"), with an annual
   ST-BDR claim after a § 166 write-off. G.L. c. 64H § 33 and 830 CMR
   64H.1.4 are blocked (sources).

## S0-3: Step 0's verbs

- each settlement in both files: `shop accounting export` (QuickBooks:
  one file; Xero: an invoices file and a credit-notes file);
- an export of a range with settlements and no invoices: the same

  settlement-rule set`, listed by `shop tax status` (Q2);

## S0-4: the questions, for the operator

The worked example is 373's own test case, because it carries D11's cent:
covered labour 15000 + parts 8000 = 23000, claim tax 500 (6.25 % on
parts), **claimed 23500**. Paid in full, row 373 books, in cents:

| account | Dr | Cr |
|---|---|---|
| A/R (provider) | 23500 | |
| labour income | | 15000 |
| parts income | | 8000 |
| sales tax payable | | 500 |

### Q1. What each settlement books

**Part approval at 20000, shortfall 3500.** `generate_shortfall_invoice`:
pre-tax 23000 × 3500 / 23500 = 3425.53 → 3426, split 2234 labour + 1192
parts; the customer's tax 1192 × 6.25 % = 74.5 → 75; the customer owes
3501. The claim's tax inside the shortfall is 3500 − 3426 = 74. D11's
difference is 75 − 74 = +1.
**Denial, shortfall 23500:** pre-tax 23000 (15000 + 8000), customer tax
500, customer owes 23500; difference 0.

**1A — reverse and re-bill (recommended).** The same shape in both files.
- *Billed*, QuickBooks: two journals.
  - `…-W<claim>-CR`: Dr labour 2234, Dr parts 1192, Dr sales tax 74;
    Cr A/R (provider) 3500.
  - the shortfall invoice, as an ordinary invoice: Dr A/R (customer)
    3501; Cr labour 2234, Cr parts 1192, Cr sales tax 75.
- *Billed*, Xero: a credit note to the provider (labour −2234, parts
  −1192 with its tax 74), and the shortfall invoice to the customer
  (labour 2234, parts 1192 with tax 75), in the invoices file.
- *Absorbed*, QuickBooks: one journal `…-W<claim>-CR`: Dr absorbed
  account 3500; Cr A/R (provider) 3500.
- *Absorbed*, Xero: a credit note to the provider, one line, −3500 to the
  absorbed account at the rate the shop maps (a no-tax rate), as Xero's
  bad-debt page books it (X5).
- **Where it ends**, against paid in full:

| | revenue | tax payable | A/R provider | A/R customer | absorbed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| paid in full | 23000 | 500 | 23500 | — | — |
| part approval, billed | 23000 | 501 | 20000 | 3501 | — |
| part approval, absorbed | 23000 | 500 | 20000 | — | 3500 |
| denied, billed | 23000 | 500 | 0 | 23500 | — |
| denied, absorbed | 23000 | 500 | 0 | — | 23500 |

  Revenue is never counted twice: the credit reverses exactly what the
  shortfall invoice re-books. Tax payable moves only by D11's cent, to
  what the customer was actually charged.
- Every journal has one A/R line, as today (S0-2 item 2).
- By hand: QuickBooks, apply each `-CR` credit to the claim's journal in
  Receive payment (Q2); Xero, approve the drafts and allocate each credit
  note to the provider's invoice (X1, X3).

**1B — move the receivable (QuickBooks only).** *Billed*: one journal,
Dr A/R (customer) 3501; Cr A/R (provider) 3500; Cr sales tax 1. No
income lines, so fewer rows. But it puts two A/R names in one journal,
which Intuit's import page neither allows nor forbids (Q1), and it
cannot be mirrored in Xero, which has no journal to the receivable in an
invoice import: the two files would book the same settlement differently.
*Absorbed*: as 1A.

**Recommendation: 1A**, one shape in both files, inside what the vendors'
pages state.

### Q2. The tax on an absorbed shortfall

**What the sources settle (Massachusetts):** the tax charged on the claim
stays owed. T1: bad debts "may not be subtracted from gross receipts on a
vendor's sales or use tax return"; relief is a yearly ST-BDR claim once
the account is written off under IRC § 166. Nothing read says a denial
or short payment reverses the sale (LR 79-19 taxes the parts "whether or
not the charges are partially or fully covered"). **Not settled:**
whether a part approval is a bad debt or a price reduction (830 CMR
64H.1.4, blocked), and whether use tax is due on parts given away under a
claim that carried no tax (LR 00-10's statute suggests tax on the shop's
cost; that would raise the tax, and nothing read rules on it).

- **2A — the tax stays owed, recorded per jurisdiction as a reading
  (recommended).** A new rule table, `tax_settlement_rules`, the shape of
  373's `tax_warranty_rules`: jurisdiction, `absorbed_tax` =
  `stays_owed`, basis `reading`, validity 12 months, source, notes. One
  row for US-MA citing T1, its notes naming ST-BDR and the two unsettled
  points, for the shop's accountant to confirm. The export books an
  absorbed shortfall whole (its tax inside it) to the absorbed account,
  and refuses an absorbed claim that carried tax when the shop's
  jurisdiction has no reading on record, naming `shop tax settlement-rule
  set`, as 281 and 373 refuse an unknown rule. Only `stays_owed` is
  allowed; a jurisdiction whose sources say otherwise needs its own row
  (no code for a reading nobody has). Lowers no tax.
- **2B — 2A, plus a `reversed` treatment** (credit income and tax back,
  as a credit note to the sales accounts would) for a jurisdiction that
  records it. Ships code no recorded reading uses, and lowers tax where it
  is chosen; no source read supports it.
- **2C — no record:** always book the tax as owed, with the CLI's note
  naming the accountant. Hard to tell from hard-coding one state's
  answer, which the operator ruled out.

Separately, for 2A: file a finding for **use tax on parts given away**
under a denied, absorbed claim that carried no tax, since the export
holds no cost basis and no source read in full settles it.

### Q3. Where an absorbed shortfall is booked

- **3A — a new account kind, `absorbed` (recommended)**, "warranty
  shortfall absorbed". Migration 083 rebuilds `accounting_accounts` for
  its CHECK (live 0 rows, so no row changes). Before its first export with
  an absorbed claim, the shop maps it:
  - QuickBooks: `--kind absorbed --account "<an expense account, e.g.
    Bad debts>"`;
  - Xero: `--kind absorbed --account <code> --tax-type "<a no-tax rate,
    e.g. Tax Exempt>"`.
  The export refuses an absorbed settlement without it, naming the
  command, as it does for any unmapped kind today.
- **3B — a table of its own** (`accounting_settlement_accounts`): no
  rebuild, but a second mapping table and command for one account.
- **3C — reuse `misc`:** books the loss as shop-supplies income. Wrong
  account.

083 also has to rebuild `accounting_exports`, whose `invoice_count > 0`
would refuse to record a range with settlements and no invoices (S0-1);
live 0 rows. It adds `settlement_count` and `accounting_export_settlements`
(which export carried which settlement, per target).

### Q5 (a fork the sources raised). The tax on a Xero credit note

The page does not state `TaxAmount`'s sign (X1).
- **5A — the credit-notes file is tax-inclusive (recommended).** No
  `TaxAmount` column. Each line's negative `UnitAmount` is its share
  including its share of the claim's tax (parts −(1192 + 74) = −1266);
  Xero works out the tax inside from `TaxType`. Every statement used is
  on the page ("Enter all prices in this file as either tax inclusive or
  tax exclusive"; "The Total and Tax Amount columns will be calculated
  automatically"). The credit note's total is exact, so it clears the
  provider's invoice to the cent; the tax inside is Xero's rounding,
  which can differ from the claim's 74 by a cent, and the CLI prints the
  expected tax per credit note to check before approving.
- **5B — tax-exclusive with `TaxAmount`:** exact tax, but its sign is a
  guess past the page. Not offered unless the operator has a source.

## S0-5: decisions taken here, not stops

- **D1. A settlement whose claim was never exported to that target**
  refuses the export, naming the claim and the day to export from:
  booking a credit against a receivable the books never held would leave
  the provider owing a negative amount. A claim exported in the same run
  is fine; its journal or invoice comes first in the file.
- **D2. Numbers:** the credit (QuickBooks journal, Xero credit note) is
  `<claim number>-CR`, from the claim's `<invoice number>-W<claim id>`;
  the billed shortfall invoice keeps its own number. Each is exported
  once per target (`accounting_export_settlements`), by the shop's day of
  `settled_at`, parsed (377).
- **D3. The Xero credit notes go in a second file** beside `--out`,
  `<name>_credit_notes.csv`, written only when there are credit notes;
  the invoices file is written only when it has rows. The CLI names each
  file and what it holds.
- **D4. A run with no settlements writes what it writes today**, the
  same bytes (a test compares them).
- **D5. Command line only;** no API model moves, so no mobile session.
