### 2026-09-08 — Step 0, capped research, close

- **Zero triple-sport content** across 795 entries — the only hit was my
  own boundary sentence in the Tiger file. But the naked segment is
  crowded (MT-09, Z900, GSX-S, Monster), so the designation bar and a
  corpus-wide counter-assertion both apply.
- **The Daytona had no row at all.** This row's detail reads "triple
  naked sport" and the Daytona is a faired supersport, so the row's own
  scope excluded it; 226, 227, 229 and 230 cover the twins, the Tigers,
  the pre-Hinckley bikes and electrical. Two compat rows already named
  it. **Fourth consecutive block-opening row omission** after the 690
  Duke, the 790 Bonneville and the Tiger 1050 — the pattern is that the
  roadmap's model lists were written from model *names*, and every make
  has a machine whose name does not announce which list it belongs to.
- **Research was capped, dropped and reinstated inside one phase.** The
  user asked for a ~6-agent cap after raising that long unsupervised
  runs are a real concern, then dropped workflows entirely; the capped
  run was stopped mid-flight and I began writing from general knowledge.
  The consequence was immediate — no recall numbers, no publication
  numbers, no cited intervals, and I said so rather than inventing them
  — and on that basis workflows were reinstated.
- **Then research contradicted two of the entries I had just written.**
  The Daytona 675 and Street Triple 675 share an engine only to 2012;
  from 2013 the Daytona took a bigger bore and shorter stroke with
  higher compression while the Street Triple kept the original, so they
  are different engines rather than different calibrations. And trim
  suffixes are not reliably engine-neutral: the **2017–2022 Street
  Triple S is a 660**, not a 765. Both were written confidently and both
  would have sent someone to order wrong parts. That is the clearest
  evidence yet for the research pass, and it cost half what the previous
  runs did.
- **The provenance split is asserted rather than described.** Three
  entries stay `model-generated`, eight are `service-manual`, and a test
  forbids any unsourced entry from carrying a recall number, publication
  number or interval figure.
- **Both refuters caught the same misreading of a recall form** — the
  candidate model list read as the affected population, when the form's
  unit table shows one model and the rest at zero. What survived is
  better than the claim: the regulator's **structured model index lists
  models with zero units and omits the affected one**, so a model-name
  query never returns that campaign. The entry teaches the frame-number
  habit.
- **Eleven entries**, led by a **Street Triple ABS campaign where
  anti-lock can be lost with no warning lamp** — rated critical because
  the ordinary dash check gives a false pass. Also two 2013 transmission
  campaigns with non-overlapping frame ranges, the **Speed Triple 1200
  radiator-fan recall whose follow-up exists because the first repair
  may not have been effective** (second superseded-remedy instance after
  the Bonneville alternator connector), a negative finding that no
  recall touches the 675 valve train, valve intervals at 12,000 miles
  except the Speed Triple 1200 at 20,000, and Daytona charging failures
  split between a campaign and a wider owner-reported pattern.
- **Ninth and tenth instances of my selector confusing mention with
  use**: a regex read "for 2013–2017 they are **not** the same engine"
  as a sharing claim, and read "do not apply the general rule that trims
  share an engine without checking" as one too. Both fixed by asserting
  what entries must say rather than pattern-matching what they must not,
  with a guard-on-the-guard for the second.
- **Cost after the cap: 6 agents, 827K subagent tokens, 36 minutes** —
  roughly half of phases 225–227, with progress reported during the run.
