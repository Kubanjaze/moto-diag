**Is `customer_notifications` the communication log? No.** It is the
queue of outbound template messages:
- `event` is CHECK-limited to ten system events (`wo_opened` …
  `approval_requested`); `channel` to `email`, `sms`, `in_app`;
- a service writer cannot record a phone call, a conversation at the
  counter, or anything the customer said; there is no inbound direction
  and no free text outside a template;
- nothing sends: it is "queue-only" by its docstring.

A log adds a dated free-text record of each contact, either direction, any
channel (phone, in person, email, text, other), optionally tied to a work
order, and one timeline per customer that shows it beside the
notifications.

**A defect found on the way.** `shop customer bikes` and `shop customer
show` print `?` in the Relationship column for every bike: the query
aliases the column `cb_relationship` and the table reads `relationship`.
Reproduced on a scratch database (link as owner → `?`). It is on this
row's surface, so the build fixes it as a bug fix with a test.

## S0-3. Row 279, parts inventory with reorder points — the verbs

| verb | today |

- **No order quantity is stored**, only the reorder point. A PO needs one.
  Decision: add `reorder_quantity` (the quantity to order when stock is at
  or below the point). An item with none set is listed as not ordered, with
  the reason; no quantity is invented.

- **An item already on an open PO** (draft or sent) is not ordered again.
- A PO is generated locally, one per vendor, as a draft; `sent` is a
  status the user sets after sending it by their own means (nothing leaves
  the machine); `receive` adds the ordered quantities to stock.
- `inventory_items` has 0 live rows, so a new column changes no row.

## S0-4. Row 280, warranty validity and claims — the verbs

- **Validity lookup:** `warranties` holds coverage per bike (type,
  provider, start and end date, mileage limit). The lookup states, per
  coverage, valid or not on a date and at a mileage, and why ("ended
  2026-03-01", "over 12,000 mi"). A limit that is not recorded is shown as
  not recorded, never as a pass. The mileage comes from `--mileage`, else
  `vehicles.mileage`, else it is unknown.
- **Claim records:** no table exists. A claim ties a warranty to a work
  order, with a status (`draft`, `submitted`, `approved`, `denied`,
  `paid`), a claim number from the maker when there is one, the amounts
  claimed and approved, and dates. `warranties.claim_count` is kept in step
  by `increment_claim_count` when a claim is opened.
- **Printable claim packet:** a plain-text document from stored data: shop,
  customer, bike and VIN, coverage, work order, issues, parts, labour hours
  and time entries, the claim's status. Written to a file or printed.
- **Submission to the maker** is row 362, paused: the ledger change is in
  this commit.

## S0-5. Row 290, financial reporting — the costs the data holds

The row promises "P&L per mechanic, per bay, per customer,
monthly/quarterly/annual". **Revenue is stored; costs are not.**

| P&L line | stored? | where |
|---|---|---|
| revenue | **yes** | `invoices` + `invoice_line_items`, typed `labor`, `parts`, `diagnostic`, `misc` (shop supplies); tax separate |
| to a customer | **yes** | `invoices.customer_id` |
| to a mechanic | **yes** | `invoices.work_order_id` → `work_orders.assigned_mechanic_user_id`; hours by `work_order_time_entries.user_id` |
| to a bay | **yes, by allocation** | `bay_schedule_slots` (bay ↔ work order, scheduled and actual times); a work order can use several bays |
| parts cost | **no** | a part's only figure is `parts.typical_cost_cents` or the line's `unit_cost_cents_override`, and `generate_invoice_for_wo` bills the part at exactly that figure. The stored "cost" *is* the price, so a parts margin computed from it is 0 by construction. `inventory_items.unit_cost` is a purchase cost, but inventory items are a separate catalogue with no link to a work order's parts |
| a mechanic's labour cost | **no** | no pay rate anywhere: `users`, `shop_members`, `work_order_assignments` have none. `labor_rates.hourly_rate` is the rate charged to customers (the invoice's labour line), and it is empty live |
| overheads (rent, utilities, tools) | **no** | no expense table |
| a bay's cost | **no** | `shop_bays` has type and weight limit only |

**So a P&L per mechanic, per bay and per customer cannot be computed from
stored data.** Every cost line would be zero or invented. This is the
stop the prompt set: the options are below.

Constraint on any option: the API serves `revenue_rollup` and
`dashboard_snapshot` (`/v1/shops/{id}/analytics/revenue`, `/snapshot`).
Changing `RevenueRollup` or `DashboardSnapshot` changes a response schema
and turns gate 11 red. The report is a new function and a new command;
the two existing models are not touched, and no route is added.

## S0-6. Row 291, estimate vs actual variance — the verbs

| verb | data |
|---|---|
| labour time variance | `work_orders.estimated_hours` vs `actual_hours`. The existing `labor-accuracy` compares only the AI estimates in `labor_estimates` (0 live rows) and ignores a work order's own estimate |
| parts cost variance | `work_orders.estimated_parts_cost_cents` vs the parts actually on the work order (installed and received lines, at the figure the invoice bills) |
| quote accuracy | no quote amount is stored. The estimate in money is the estimated hours at the labour rate plus the estimated parts; compared with the invoice's subtotal before tax, at the rate the invoice charged |

Decision: 291 is computable. A per-work-order variance report
(`shop analytics variance`), with the shop-wide totals; work orders with
no estimate are counted and named as such, not scored.

**A defect found on the way, filed as F182.** The estimate a customer is
sent (`estimate_ready`) is `estimated_hours × 100.0`, a hard-coded rate,
with the parts left out. Measured on a scratch database: 2.0 hours and
$549.91 of parts renders "$200.00". No test covers the figure. Quote
accuracy cannot be measured against a quote that was never the estimate,
so the plan fixes it in this batch: the estimate is the estimated hours at
the shop's labour rate plus the estimated parts, and when no rate is known
the notification is refused, never invented. Whether this fix belongs in
this batch is the operator's to veto (question 2 below).

## Questions for the operator

### 1. Row 290: the P&L needs costs the data does not hold (a real fork)

**(A) Revenue reporting now; the P&L paused until costs are recorded.**
- Ships `motodiag shop analytics revenue` with `--by mechanic|bay|customer`
  and `--period month|quarter|year`: revenue split into labour, parts,
  diagnostic and supplies (tax apart), invoices and hours worked per group.
  Bay revenue is allocated by the slot hours a work order spent in each
  bay.
- Row 290 is rewritten to "revenue reporting"; the P&L goes to a new ⏸️
  row, "paused until the shop records its costs".
- No schema for 290. Smallest.

**(B) Record direct costs; a gross-margin P&L per mechanic, bay and
customer, and a shop P&L per period. (Recommended.)**
- **A mechanic's labour cost rate**, per shop member, dated
  (`shop member cost-rate`), new table. Labour cost = logged time entries
  × the rate in force on the day.
- **A part's purchase cost, apart from its billed price**, recorded on a
  work order's part line (`shop parts-needs cost`), new table keyed by the
  line. The invoice keeps billing what it bills now.
- **Shop expenses** per month by category (rent, utilities, tools …),
  `shop expense add/list`, new table.
- **The report:** per mechanic, per bay and per customer, revenue − parts
  purchase cost − labour cost = gross margin. Per period for the whole
  shop, gross margin − expenses = net. **Expenses are never allocated to
  a mechanic, bay or customer**; that would need a rule this data does not
  give. Where a cost was not recorded, the line reads "not recorded" and
  the margin is not computed; nothing is shown as zero.
- All new tables: no existing live row changes.

**(C) As B, and expenses allocated to mechanic, bay and customer by labour
hours**, so every level shows a net figure.
- The allocation rule (by labour hours) is a choice, and every net figure
  below the shop depends on it.
