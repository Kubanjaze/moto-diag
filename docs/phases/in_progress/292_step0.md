## S0-4: three hand-offs that do not exist, or do not hold

**H1. The Xero export gives untaxed labour a share of the tax.**
`accounting/export.py:331` spreads the invoice's tax over every line in
proportion to its amount (`spread_tax`, Phase 275). Phase 281 then made
tax fall on some line types only, and records them on the invoice
(`taxed_line_types`). The export never reads that column. On a
Massachusetts invoice, labour is not taxable, yet its Xero row carries
380 of the 575 cents of tax, and the parts row 195 where the invoice's
parts tax is 575. The sum still equals the invoice's tax, which is why
275's and 281's tests pass. QuickBooks is right: it credits the tax
account once, with the invoice's tax. No test covers a mixed invoice in
the Xero file.

**H2. A warranty claim and the invoice do not know about each other.**
The claim links the work order; the invoice is built from the same work
order. Nothing passes from one to the other. In the dry walk, the
covered repair (a valid comprehensive warranty, a claim open on the work
order) was invoiced to the customer in full, 27773 cents, and paid by
them. The claim's amount stayed "not recorded": it is typed by hand
(`--claimed-cents`), and nothing derives it from the work order's
labour and parts.

**H3. Check-in creates no intake, and none can be attached later.**
`appointment check-in` without `--wo` opens a work order with no
intake. `intake_visit_id` is not updatable (`work_order_repo.py:85`:
"cancel the WO and recreate"), and the checked-in appointment cannot be
checked in again. So book → intake → work order holds only in one
order: intake, then `work-order create --intake`, then `check-in --wo`.
The walk uses that order. In the other order the claim packet falls back
from the intake's mileage to the bike's recorded mileage.

## S0-5: decisions taken here, not stops

- **D1. The mechanic is user 1.** No command creates a user. Migration
  005 seeds user 1 ("system"), and every phase since has used it as the
  operator's user. `shop member add --user 1` is a real command, and
  `slots` needs an active member. Nothing is seeded, so this is not a
  finding.
- **D2. The clock.** The walk runs at 2026-10-15 12:00 in
  America/New_York (16:00 UTC), a Thursday. Every `motodiag` module's
  `datetime` and `date` names are replaced for the walk, as Phase 370's
  `frozen_clock` does for two. The Stripe SDK's webhook clock is set to
  the same instant (273's `freeze_webhook_clock`), and the event is
  signed at that instant. `CURRENT_TIMESTAMP` column defaults keep the
  real clock; the walk reads none of them. The first assertion is that
  the invoice is dated the frozen day, so a freeze that does not take
  hold fails the gate on any other day.
- **D3. Two card channels, one cash job.** An invoice can be paid
  once. The walk runs twice, on fresh databases: once paid by `pay-link`
  and once by `terminal pay`. In each, a second job on the same shop is
  paid by `mark-paid`, and both are exported in one file per target.
  The second job carries the expired warranty case.
