## What shipped

- **The rule, and how a user reaches it:**
  - `shop warranty add --payer {maker_with_bike,other,shop_contract}`, or
    `shop warranty update N --payer … --provider …`;
  - `shop warranty claim cover CLAIM --labour-hours H --part ROW[=QTY]`
    (or `--none`) after the work. The invoice refuses while a claim on
    its work order has no coverage recorded;
  - `shop invoice generate` leaves the covered hours and quantities off
    the customer's lines and prices them for the claim. The amount
    claimed = covered lines + the claim's tax, in integer cents, never
    typed (`--claimed-cents` is gone);
  - the claim's tax comes from `tax_warranty_rules` for the payer on the
    invoice date. In Massachusetts: someone else's plan, 6.25% of the
    covered parts, on the claim; a maker's warranty with the bike, or the
    shop's own contract, nothing on the claim. **A reading, to be
    confirmed by the shop's accountant before a real warranty job.**
    Other jurisdictions: `shop tax warranty-rule set`; without a rule the
    invoice is refused;
  - `shop warranty claim settle CLAIM --bill-customer | --absorb` for a
    claim denied or paid short. Only this writes a second invoice on a
    work order;
  - `shop warranty claim packet` lists the covered lines and the amount;
  - `shop accounting export`: QuickBooks carries the claim as a journal
    entry with an A/R debit in the provider's name, and Xero as an
    invoice to the provider.
